3 — THE FOUR FORCES

Twenty-one parameters collapse into four named forces with named zones. You choose a world, not a value — and the window tells you which world you just chose. Raw controls stay, one fold down.

4 — THE FATE DECK

Not settings you adjust — cards you play at the world. Each is a named, illustrated bundle of parameter changes with a stated consequence. Timed cards revert themselves, which makes “the era we survived” expressible for the first time.

Authorship of events, not authorship of settings. “I played the Long Winter and the movers went extinct” is a story; “I set lifespanMultiplier to 300” is not. Guardrail: every card is a pressure, never a result — cards change what the world rewards, they never place or remove specific organisms. Pure selection survives intact.

5 — THE TEST CHAMBER

Answers the question the player actually has: what will this do? A forked copy of the world runs the pending settings 2000 ticks ahead, three seeds deep, and reports the spread. Nothing touches the live sim until you commit.

Tuning becomes experimentation: hypothesis, forecast, commit. It also teaches the sim — after a dozen forecasts you have an intuition for what abundance does, which is the best thing this window could possibly give a player. Cost is real: fast-forwarding a detached world without stuttering the live one is a Web Worker job.

Recommended path: 2 first (nearly free, fixes the worst problem), then 1 as the visual layer, then pick an identity — 3 to stay a tuning surface, 4 to become a play surface, 5 to become a laboratory. Full reasoning in evolution-window-overhauls.md.
